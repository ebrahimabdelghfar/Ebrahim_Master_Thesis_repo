% === IV. EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS =============================================
\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Experimental setup}

All experiments are conducted in simulation. The plant is the Formula AI
vehicle of Section~\ref{subsec:platform} in CARLA~\cite{carla2017}, driven
through \bridge{} on a ROS~2 Humble workspace; the CARLA server runs in
synchronous mode at a \SI{0.033}{\second} fixed physics step. Identification
data is collected over \SI{30}{\second} of pure-pursuit driving at
\SI{50}{\hertz}, matching the baseline's data budget~\cite{ontrack2024}.
Ground truth for tire forces, slip angles and normal loads is the simulator's
own per-wheel telemetry.

\subsubsection{Reference trajectory}
\label{subsubsec:raceline}
Every run follows the same optimised raceline, shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:raceline}. It was produced with the TUM
\texttt{global\_race\-trajectory\_optimization}
package~\cite{tumracetraj}, vendored in the simulator workspace, using its
minimum-curvature formulation~\cite{heilmeier2020} on a recorded centreline of
the CARLA \texttt{silverstone} circuit. The result is a closed
\SI{5830}{\meter} lap of 2916 waypoints at \SI{2.0}{\meter} spacing, with a
reference speed of \SIrange{14.90}{46.02}{\meter\per\second} and a minimum
corner radius of \SI{18.7}{\meter} ($|\kappa|_{\max}=\SI{0.0536}{\per\meter}$).

Two of the optimiser's inputs matter for Section~\ref{subsec:negative} and are
therefore stated explicitly. Its vehicle configuration assumes a mass of
\SI{204}{\kilo\gram}, against the \SI{269.6}{\kilo\gram} the physics engine
actually simulates (Table~\ref{tab:vehicle}). More consequentially, its
\texttt{ggv} envelope declares a \emph{constant} lateral capability of
\SI{12.0}{\meter\per\second\squared} at every speed. The resulting profile
saturates that envelope exactly---the peak demand
$\max(v_{x}^{2}|\kappa|)$ over the lap is \SI{12.00}{\meter\per\second\squared},
i.e.\ \SI{1.22}{\g}---so the trajectory is feasible \emph{by construction} for
the vehicle the optimiser was told about, and only for that vehicle. Measured
against the plant's actual steady-state ceiling of
$\approx\SI{9.8}{\meter\per\second\squared}$, \SI{11.9}{\percent} of the lap is
infeasible (Fig.~\ref{fig:raceline}c).

\subsection{The failure mode, reproduced}
\label{subsec:failure}

Running the baseline pipeline unmodified on this platform yields
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{C\_Pf} &= [5.2874,\; 1.5536,\; \mathbf{0.4},\; \mathbf{1.0}],\\
\mathrm{C\_Pr} &= [19.3236,\; \mathbf{1.2},\; 0.4046,\; -0.1292],
\end{align*}
with the bold entries sitting exactly on their box constraints and $B_{r}$
just under its upper bound: five of eight coefficients on or against a rail.
The implied physics is not merely inaccurate but qualitatively wrong---a grip
ceiling of \SI{3.95}{\meter\per\second\squared} (\SI{0.40}{\g}) and a front
axle stiffness of \SI{4012}{\newton\per\radian}, meaning the model believes it
needs $\approx\SI{0.30}{\radian}$ of front slip to generate \SI{1}{\g}, well
beyond the vehicle's \SI{0.2793}{\radian} steering limit. The model
essentially believes the car cannot corner.

The decisive experiment is an offline round-trip with the residual network
zeroed, so that only the rollout is under test: a synthetic $D=1.5$ tire,
rolled out at \SI{4}{\meter\per\second} and re-fitted, returns
$D_{f}=0.753$, $D_{r}=0.737$ with three coefficients railed; the same tire
rolled out at \SIrange{8}{20}{\meter\per\second} returns
$D=\numrange{1.40}{1.61}$ with nothing railed. Since the tire is known
exactly and the residual model is absent, this isolates the rollout
configuration as the cause and confirms \eqref{eq:mureach}.

\subsection{Results after correction}
\label{subsec:corrected}

With the six defects of Table~\ref{tab:defects} corrected, three consecutive
live identifications return no coefficient on a bound. Peak friction
coefficients land at $D_{f},D_{r}\in[1.05,1.18]$ against the reference
\num{1.009}/\num{1.002} of Table~\ref{tab:ref}; axle cornering stiffnesses
land at $C_{f}\approx\SI{21}{\kilo\newton\per\radian}$ and
$C_{r}\approx\SI{20}{\kilo\newton\per\radian}$ against the simulator's
measured \SIrange{17}{19}{\kilo\newton\per\radian}; the sign of the
understeer gradient is correct ($l_{f}C_{f}<l_{r}C_{r}$) on every run,
matching the measured plant; and one-step prediction on held-out data gives
$R^{2}=\num{0.97}$ for $v_{y}$ and $R^{2}=\num{0.99}$ for $\omega$. The
peak-friction drift across the six co-identification iterations is removed:
where the unbounded sweep walked $D$ from \num{1.01} to \num{1.87}, the
bounded sweep with a frozen prior settles inside $[1.05,1.18]$.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{subsec:ablation}

Two ablation axes are exposed by the implementation and should be reported in
the final version of this paper. Neither has yet been run to completion, and
we mark them as such rather than reporting placeholder numbers.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Residual architecture}: \texttt{baseline} MLP vs
\texttt{wide} vs \texttt{physics\_inputs} vs \texttt{ensemble} vs
\texttt{s4} (S4D), scored on one-step and 10-step RMSE and on the identified
coefficients. The relevant comparison in the literature
is~\cite{visionsysid2026}, which reports a lateral-force RMSE reduction
exceeding \SI{60}{\percent} for an S4 residual over conventional
architectures; we should expect a smaller effect here because our sequences
are short and our residual is dominated by a correctable measurement bias
rather than by unmodelled temporal dynamics.
\item \textbf{Loss mode}: \texttt{mse} vs \texttt{physics\_informed}
(dynamics-consistency $+$ symmetry $+$ smoothness terms), on the same
dataset, comparing one-step metrics, per-iteration coefficient trajectories,
and closed-loop tracking.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Negative result: the raceline, not the model}
\label{subsec:negative}

With the identifier corrected, the closed-loop leg of the validation
(Section~\ref{subsec:validation}, level 3) does \emph{not} complete, and the
reason is instructive. The identified model reports a grip ceiling of
\SIrange{1.02}{1.16}{\g}; the measured plant sustains $\approx\SI{1.0}{\g}$ in
steady state; the raceline used in this study demands
$\max(v_{x}^{2}|\kappa|)=\SI{12.00}{\meter\per\second\squared}=\SI{1.22}{\g}$
($P_{99}=\SI{11.90}{\meter\per\second\squared}$). The admissibility gate of
Section~\ref{subsec:gates} therefore rejects every identification---correctly.
The trajectory is infeasible for this vehicle, and the corrected identifier is
what made that visible.

The cause is not obscure, and it is worth naming because it is a failure mode
any model-based stack can inherit. As Section~\ref{subsubsec:raceline}
records, the trajectory optimiser was handed a \texttt{ggv} envelope asserting
a constant \SI{12.0}{\meter\per\second\squared} of lateral capability and a
vehicle mass of \SI{204}{\kilo\gram}. It did its job: the profile saturates
that envelope to the last digit. The envelope was simply not this vehicle's.
The identification pipeline and the trajectory were parameterised from two
different descriptions of the same car, and nothing in the stack compared them
until the gate did.

We deliberately do not loosen the gate. The quantitative consequence for
trajectory regeneration is: the tightest corner has
$|\kappa|=\SI{0.0536}{\per\meter}$, which at the measured grip permits
$\sqrt{g\mu/|\kappa|}=\SI{13.5}{\meter\per\second}$
(\SIrange{13.7}{14.6}{\meter\per\second} at the identified ceiling of
\SIrange{1.02}{1.16}{\g}); capping the speed profile at
\SI{13}{\meter\per\second} brings peak demand to
\SI{9.05}{\meter\per\second\squared} (\SI{0.92}{\g}), whereas
\SI{15}{\meter\per\second} returns it to \SI{12.00}{\meter\per\second\squared}.
Regenerating the raceline with the \emph{measured} vehicle
($m=\SI{269.6}{\kilo\gram}$, $\mu_{\mathrm{peak}}=1.0$,
$v_{\max}\approx\SIrange{13}{14}{\meter\per\second}$) is the prerequisite for
the closed-loop experiment, and is the immediate next step
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).
