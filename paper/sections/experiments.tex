% === VI. EXPERIMENTS AND RESULTS =============================================
\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

All experiments run on the architecture of Section~\ref{sec:platform}: the
Formula AI vehicle in CARLA~\cite{carla2017} driven through ROS~2 Humble, the
server in synchronous mode at a \SI{0.033}{\second} fixed physics step. The
identifier collects \SI{30}{\second} of data at \SI{50}{\hertz} and
re-identifies from the newest buffer, matching the baseline's data
budget~\cite{ontrack2025}. Ground truth for forces, slip angles and normal
loads is the simulator's own per-wheel telemetry, which the identifier never
reads.

\subsubsection{Surface}
Every run uses a \emph{static} surface: the per-wheel coefficient is held at
its configured \num{1.0}, and the road-surface factor of \num{0.70} PhysX
applies on top of it makes the effective coefficient \num{0.70}, constant to
the last digit the simulator reports over all \num{10744} and \num{12025}
force-validation samples of the two runs. It is deliberately \emph{not} the
$\mu=\num{1.05}$ surface the tire prior was measured on
(Section~\ref{subsubsec:priorprovenance}), so a pipeline that cannot move off
its prior has nowhere to hide. It remains a \emph{single} surface: time-varying
friction is out of scope, and the adaptivity claim a friction sweep would
support is not made (Section~\ref{subsec:limits}).

\subsubsection{Reference trajectory}
\label{subsubsec:raceline}
Both runs follow the same optimised raceline (Fig.~\ref{fig:raceline}),
produced with the TUM global race trajectory optimisation
package~\cite{tumracetraj} under its minimum-curvature
formulation~\cite{heilmeier2020} on a recorded centreline of the CARLA
silverstone circuit: a closed \SI{5828.2}{\meter} lap of \num{2916} waypoints
at \SI{2.0}{\meter} spacing, reference speed
\SIrange{9.62}{25.78}{\meter\per\second}, minimum corner radius
\SI{18.7}{\meter} ($|\kappa|_{\max}=\SI{0.0536}{\per\meter}$). Its peak lateral
demand is $\max(v_{x}^{2}|\kappa|)=\SI{4.98}{\meter\per\second\squared}$
(\SI{0.51}{\g}), which saturates the flat
\SI{5.0}{\meter\per\second\squared} \texttt{ggv} envelope the optimiser was
given and stays at roughly \SI{73}{\percent} of the \SI{0.70}{\g} this
surface sustains. It replaces the earlier trajectory, whose \SI{1.22}{\g}
demand the admissibility gate of Section~\ref{sec:integration} rejected against
the same plant; that regeneration, not a relaxed gate, unblocked the
closed-loop runs.

\subsubsection{The two configurations}
\label{subsubsec:scenarios}
Two configurations of the same pipeline are run back to back on that
trajectory, three timed laps each, with an untimed out-lap from the spawn point
to the raceline's $s=0$. They differ in the five settings of
Table~\ref{tab:scenarios} and in nothing else; vehicle, map, trajectory,
controller, data budget, loop count and box constraints are shared.
\texttt{Baseline-NN-MSE} is the inherited configuration (MLP residual, one-step
MSE objective, local trust-region fit, no friction warm start, undamped update)
evaluated at full scale on the corrected rollout, so the comparison isolates
those five settings rather than reproducing the published pipeline in full
(Section~\ref{subsec:baselinecmp}); \texttt{Ours} is the shipped configuration.
Since the five settings move together, what follows is a configuration
comparison, not a per-factor ablation, which Section~\ref{subsec:ablations}
supplies offline. Nor is it a test of this paper's central contribution: the
excitation-aware rollout is active in \emph{both} arms, so the closed-loop
comparison measures the five secondary settings on top of it and the correction
itself is evidenced in Section~\ref{subsec:sweep}.

\subsubsection{Provenance of the tire prior}
\label{subsubsec:priorprovenance}
The prior $\phip^{0}$ both arms start from, and that the Tikhonov term of
\eqref{eq:fitobj} pulls toward, is the measured tire of Table~\ref{tab:ref},
obtained by fitting \eqref{eq:mf} to CARLA's own per-wheel telemetry over a
dedicated limit-excitation run that reached $\alpha=\SI{0.37}{\radian}$, axle
$\mu=\num{1.05}$ and \SI{1.50}{\g}, by differential evolution, at
\SI{135}{\newton} front and \SI{85}{\newton} rear residual RMSE. Three
consequences follow. The prior is not independent of the validation data, since
that is the same channel the force and friction results are scored against. The
manoeuvre that produced it is one the pipeline is not allowed to run: the
raceline peaks at \SI{0.51}{\g} and never approaches the tire's peak, the
excitation deficit this paper is about. And, decisively, the prior was measured
on a surface these runs do not use: it carries $D=\num{1.009}/\num{1.002}$ and
axle slopes of \SI{13.2}{\kilo\newton\per\radian} front and
\SI{13.9}{\kilo\newton\per\radian} rear against the \num{0.70} and the
\num{17.4} and \SI{16.6}{\kilo\newton\per\radian} this surface realises
(Section~\ref{subsec:resultstire}). Returning it unchanged is a
\SI{44}{\percent} grip overstatement and a \SIrange{16}{24}{\percent} stiffness
deficit, visible in every metric of Table~\ref{tab:comparison}; to score well
an arm has to \emph{move}, in the right direction, from a prior that is wrong.

\subsubsection{What is measured}
Three families of quantity are recorded against the plant's own telemetry: the
identified tire model (coefficients, axle force, peak friction), the one-step
state prediction, and closed-loop MPC tracking. Every figure is exported with
the CSV holding its samples, and the cross-run comparison reads those CSVs
rather than re-deriving any metric, so a figure and the table cannot disagree.

\subsection{Identifiability of the peak factor against rollout excitation}
\label{subsec:sweep}

The claim of Section~\ref{subsec:excitation} concerns the rollout
configuration alone, so it is tested where the answer is known exactly rather
than in CARLA. A tire with a known peak factor $D$ is rolled out through the
synthetic sweep of stage~2 (the shipped rollout, residual network set to zero)
and fitted back with the shipped solver, \emph{unregularised} and started from
the true coefficients, so nothing it loses is lost to the prior, the start
point or a local minimum; everything it loses is lost to excitation.

Three axes are swept, rollout speed $v$, sweep amplitude
$\delta_{\mathrm{sweep}}$ and true $D$, each cell repeated over five noise
realisations. The configuration axes are swept one at a time, because
$\mu_{\mathrm{reach}}$ is a function of both and a claim that it, and not the
speed, governs the fit cannot be tested by moving the speed alone. The
repetition is over noise rather than over the solver because the noise-free fit
is exactly seed-invariant here: five multi-start seeds return the same
coefficients to four decimal places, and so does a jitter width seven times
wider. The injected noise is Gaussian on the rolled-out states at a one-step
persistence RMSE measured on CARLA runs of this vehicle,
$\sigma_{v_{y}}=\SI{0.009}{\meter\per\second}$ and
$\sigma_{\omega}=\SI{0.005}{\radian\per\second}$, and is what the intervals in
Table~\ref{tab:sweep} are computed over; the front and rear fits share one
rollout, so the interval is taken over the five realisations, not the ten fits.

The grid says three things. First, the inherited \SI{4}{\meter\per\second}
rollout is uninformative about $D$ at \emph{every} grip level tested: the
$0.70$, $1.05$, $1.40$ and $1.80$ tires all come back as $\hat D$ between
\num{0.50} and \num{0.59}, with two to seven of the eight coefficients on a
bound, so the fit reports $\mu_{\mathrm{reach}}$ and not the tire. Second, the
boundary is $\mu_{\mathrm{reach}}\gtrsim D$ and not a speed: at
\SI{6}{\meter\per\second}, where $\mu_{\mathrm{reach}}=0.96$, the $0.70$ and
$1.05$ tires are recovered to within \SI{3}{\percent} while the $1.40$ and
$1.80$ tires are \SI{13}{\percent} and \SI{30}{\percent} short. The boundary is
soft: the $1.05$ tire is recovered to \SI{1}{\percent} at
$\mu_{\mathrm{reach}}=0.96$, slightly \emph{below} its own $D$, because a
rollout stopping just short of the peak still traverses the curvature
separating $B$, $C$ and $D$. The bound predicts where the information runs out,
not a step. Above $\mu_{\mathrm{reach}}=1.7$ every tire is recovered to within
\SI{4}{\percent} with no systematic railing. Third, the failure is not the
solver's: by the seed invariance above, multi-start, a global solver or a
better start point cannot recover a coefficient the data does not contain,
which is why Section~\ref{subsec:fit} reports both as secondary. The one caveat
is at the top of the sweep, where a rollout far past the peak over-reports a
low-grip tire ($\hat D=\num{0.92}$ for a $0.70$ tire at
\SI{20}{\meter\per\second}), the Magic Formula's falling branch being fitted to
data the plant's own curve does not produce; the extrapolation bound of
Section~\ref{subsec:iterative} keeps the shipped configuration out of that
regime.

Panel (b) sweeps the other argument of \eqref{eq:mureach},
$\delta_{\mathrm{sweep}}$ from \SI{0.03}{\radian} to the shipped
$\delta_{\max}=\SI{0.54}{\radian}$ at fixed $v=\SI{15}{\meter\per\second}$.
Starving the amplitude destroys $D$ just as starving the speed does, so speed
is not the governing variable, and the claim is corrected in two ways. First,
the failure is not a common bias: an under-speeded rollout returns \emph{too
little} grip (every tire back at $\hat D\approx0.5$), an under-swept one too
much, the \num{1.05} tire coming back as \num{1.30} and the \num{1.80} tire
railing on the \num{2.0} bound at
$\delta_{\mathrm{sweep}}=\SI{0.06}{\radian}$, with two of eight coefficients on
a box edge in every cell. What the axes share is that $\hat D$ stops being a
function of the tire, the direction of the error being set by the rollout: the
signature of an unidentified parameter, not a biased one. Second, the axes do
\emph{not} collapse onto a common $\mu_{\mathrm{reach}}$, for the upper-bound
reason of Section~\ref{subsec:excitation}: \eqref{eq:mureach} uses the
Ackermann curvature and so tracks the realised demand only while understeer is
small, the realised $\max|v_{x}\omega|/g$ saturating near the tire's peak while
$\mu_{\mathrm{reach}}$ grows to \num{10.64} against a realised
\numrange{1.40}{2.18} at $v=\SI{20}{\meter\per\second}$. Read against the
realised demand, both axes behave alike: recovery sets in where the rollout
actually reaches the peak. Equation~\eqref{eq:mureach} should therefore be used
as the pipeline uses it, a cheap screening bound conclusive when it falls below
$D$, not a measure of excitation achieved.

One property bounds how all of this should be read: the residual network is set
to zero, so a Magic Formula is fitted to Magic-Formula data under exactly
matched structure. The recovery is a best case, and the real pipeline's
degradation at a given excitation is at least this bad.

\subsection{Identified tire model}
\label{subsec:resultstire}

Figure~\ref{fig:pacejka} puts both identified curves against the plant's own
curve~\eqref{eq:physx} and lists the coefficients. Three things are visible.

First, no coefficient of \texttt{Ours} rests on a box constraint in any of its
six accepted identifications; its last accepted set is
$B=\num{12.890}/\num{13.746}$ inside $[4,20]$,
$C=\num{1.240}/\num{1.248}$ inside $[1.2,2.2]$, $D=\num{0.768}$ on both axles
inside $[0.4,2.0]$ and $E=\num{-2.517}/\num{-1.836}$ inside $[-3,1]$.
\texttt{Baseline-NN-MSE} puts $D$ on its upper bound of \num{2.000} on both
axles in \emph{every} accepted identification and $E_{f}$ on its lower bound of
\num{-3.0} likewise, its first identification additionally railing $B$ on the
lower bound of \num{4.0} on both axles. A peak factor of \num{2.000} against a
plant realising \num{0.70} is less a \SI{186}{\percent} parameter error than
the box-edge signature of Section~\ref{subsec:identifiability}: the coefficient
is not identified, and no per-coefficient bounds check distinguishes that from
a fit. Two coefficients of \texttt{Ours} need care. $C$ settles \num{0.04}
above its lower bound, inside the box but close enough to report as shaped by
the constraint, consistent with its role in the $BCD$ product of
Section~\ref{subsec:bcd}. $E$ is not identified in either configuration: it
shapes only the region around and past the peak, which neither the lap nor the
bounded rollout reaches, and it is exactly what the frozen Tikhonov target of
\eqref{eq:fitobj} holds near nominal --- \texttt{Ours} ends at \num{-2.52}
against a \num{-2.00} prior, where the unregularised arm ends on the bound.

Second, the linear range is identified rather than inherited. Every slope here
is \eqref{eq:stiffness} for an identified set; for the plant it is the slope
fitted to the simulator's own per-wheel forces over
$|\alpha|\in[\num{0.005},\num{0.02}]\,\si{\radian}$, all at the static axle
loads $F_{z,f}=\SI{1373}{\newton}$, $F_{z,r}=\SI{1274}{\newton}$, so the three
are comparable. The plant realises \SI{17.4}{\kilo\newton\per\radian} front and
\SI{16.6}{\kilo\newton\per\radian} rear over this run's load range, below the
$\bar F_{z}=1$ closed-form values of Table~\ref{tab:ref} for the reason its
footnote gives. The prior carries \num{13.2} and \num{13.9},
\SI{24}{\percent} and \SI{16}{\percent} low; \texttt{Ours} returns \num{16.9}
and \num{16.8}, \SI{3.2}{\percent} low front and \SI{1.4}{\percent} high rear,
removing \SI{87}{\percent} and \SI{91}{\percent} of the prior's error. The
product $BCDF_{z}$, the only quantity the rollout fit determines
(Section~\ref{subsec:bcd}), is what moves and then holds: the first cycle takes
the front value from \SI{13.2}{\kilo\newton\per\radian} to \num{14.6}, and the
five cycles after the friction gate releases sit within
\SIrange{16.0}{16.9}{\kilo\newton\per\radian}, a spread of \SI{5}{\percent}
about a plant at \num{17.4}. \texttt{Baseline-NN-MSE} returns \num{37.9} and
\num{38.4}, \SI{118}{\percent} and \SI{132}{\percent} high, which is
$D=\num{2.0}$ multiplying a $B$ the fit chose against it.

Third, both sets keep the understeer sign ($l_{r}C_{r}>l_{f}C_{f}$) and pass
the stability gate of Section~\ref{subsec:gates}, at margins of
\SI{7.3}{\percent} for \texttt{Ours} ($l_{f}C_{f}=\num{12.45}$ against
$l_{r}C_{r}=\SI{13.36}{\kilo\newton\meter\per\radian}$) and \SI{9.1}{\percent}
for \texttt{Baseline-NN-MSE} (\num{27.98} against \num{30.52}), against
\SI{2.5}{\percent} for the plant. What separates them is the grip ceiling:
\eqref{eq:gripceiling} gives \SI{2.00}{\g} for \texttt{Baseline-NN-MSE}
against \SI{0.77}{\g} for \texttt{Ours}, a plant sustaining \SI{0.71}{\g} and a
trajectory demanding \SI{0.51}{\g}. The baseline's ceiling is a claim the plant
cannot honour by a factor of \num{2.8} and the prior's \SI{1.01}{\g} one it
cannot honour by \num{1.4}; only the identified set is admissible on the
quantity the controller consumes.

All three are readable because the lap now traverses part of the curvature:
over the force-validation samples $P_{99}(|\alpha|)=\SI{0.047}{\radian}$
(\SI{2.7}{\degree}) and $\max|\alpha|=\SI{0.102}{\radian}$ (\SI{5.8}{\degree})
against the \SI{8.0}{\degree} at which the plant's curve peaks at
$\mu=\num{0.70}$, and axle utilisation $\max|F_{y}|/F_{z}$ reaches \num{0.71}
front and \num{0.72} rear, against \num{0.49} for the same trajectory on the
$\mu=\num{1.05}$ surface of the earlier runs. This is still not a limit
manoeuvre --- the rollout reaches up the curve and the warm start carries the
independent information about $\mu$ --- but it suffices for the friction gate
to release, which on the $\mu=\num{1.05}$ runs it did not.

\subsection{Peak friction}
\label{subsec:resultsmu}

Table~\ref{tab:comparison} reports the error of the identified peak factor
against the effective friction the plant reports per wheel, and
Fig.~\ref{fig:mutimeseries} its evolution over the run. This is where the
pinned-$D$ design of Section~\ref{subsec:friction} is directly observable,
because the arm starts \SI{44}{\percent} away from the answer.

\texttt{Ours} publishes six accepted identifications. The first carries
$D_{f}=\num{1.009}$, $D_{r}=\num{1.002}$: the friction gate rejected that
buffer, so the axles held the prior they came in with, the hold-rather-than-fit
rule of Section~\ref{subsec:frictionfloor} and not a fitted value. From
$t=\SI{140.6}{\second}$ the gate releases on every subsequent cycle and the
published peak factor drops to \num{0.779}, \num{0.821}, \num{0.749},
\num{0.756} and \num{0.768}, front and rear alike, against a plant at
\num{0.700}: \SI{10.7}{\percent} high on average with a spread of \num{0.072},
from a prior that was \SI{44}{\percent} high. The whole-run $\mu$ RMSE of
Table~\ref{tab:comparison} is dominated by the first \SI{140}{\second} at the
prior; over the \SI{440}{\second} after the first release it is \num{0.078} on
both axles. \texttt{Baseline-NN-MSE} returns $D=\num{2.000}$ on both axles in
every accepted identification and never leaves the bound, a constant $\mu$
error of \num{1.300}, so \texttt{Ours} is \num{9.0}$\times$ better on the
whole-run figure and \num{16.7}$\times$ better once its warm start has released
--- a difference worth \SI{1.23}{\g} of claimed grip against a plant that
sustains \num{0.71}.

Two qualifications belong on the \texttt{Ours} column. The peak factor is
\emph{measured}, by the brush estimator on the recorded trace, then held, not
recovered by the Pacejka fit, which Section~\ref{subsec:bcd} shows cannot
recover it. And the residual \SI{10.7}{\percent} bias is one-signed, consistent
with the brush model's known tendency to over-read $\mu$ where the curve is not
fully traversed, which at $\max|F_{y}|/F_{z}=\num{0.71}$ it is not.

\subsection{Axle lateral force}
\label{subsec:resultsforce}

The identified curve is scored at the measured $(\alpha,F_{z})$ against the
plant's own lateral force, summed per axle (Table~\ref{tab:comparison};
Fig.~\ref{fig:forcehist} for the error distribution, bar charts and time series
in the supplementary material). \texttt{Ours} leads on both axles by an order
of magnitude in RMSE and in the error tails. The baseline's negative $R^{2}$
means its curve predicts the plant's axle force worse than the mean of that
force does, the force-metric reading of a set whose initial slope is more than
twice the plant's.

This is the linear-range result of Section~\ref{subsec:resultstire} seen as
force. At $P_{99}(|\alpha|)=\SI{2.7}{\degree}$ the metric is dominated by
cornering stiffness and says little about the peak, so the models separate
almost entirely on slope --- but because $D$ multiplies that slope in
\eqref{eq:stiffness}, \texttt{Baseline-NN-MSE}'s railed $D$ is the direct cause
of its force error rather than an overstatement of grip the linear range hides,
the opposite of what the earlier $\mu=\num{1.05}$ runs suggested.

\subsection{One-step state prediction}
\label{subsec:resultsstate}

Level~1 of the validation protocol is reported in Table~\ref{tab:comparison}
against a persistence baseline (next state equals last measured state) written
by the same benchmark node. \texttt{Ours} leads on both signals, by an order of
magnitude on lateral velocity ($R^{2}$ \num{0.998} against \num{0.709}) and by
a factor of two on yaw rate ($R^{2}$ \num{0.894} against \num{0.514}). In
Fig.~\ref{fig:statehist} the separation on $v_{y}$ is in the centre of the
distribution, while on $\omega$ the two share a centre and differ in tail mass.

Neither model beats persistence at the \SI{0.035}{\second} model step: the
skill scores $1-\mathrm{RMSE}/\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{pers}}$ are negative for
both configurations and both signals. We report them rather than $R^{2}$ alone
because a one-step metric on a smooth trajectory rewards not moving --- over
one \SI{0.035}{\second} step the plant's own states barely move. One-step
accuracy is therefore weak evidence for either tire model, and the gap is read
only as confirming the direction the axle-force and closed-loop comparisons
establish. The state traces against ground truth are in the supplementary
material.

\subsection{Closed-loop tracking}
\label{subsec:resultsclosedloop}

Both runs finish three timed laps with zero emergency halts and the same
finite-state sequence (bootstrap pure pursuit, running pure pursuit, handover,
running MPC), one transition of each kind and no fallback after handover, read
off each run's exported state timeline, which records the handover at
$t=\SI{79.1}{\second}$ for \texttt{Ours} and $t=\SI{64.0}{\second}$ for
\texttt{Baseline-NN-MSE} and no state change thereafter. (The aggregated
\texttt{transitions} column of the cross-run summary is not populated and is
omitted from Table~\ref{tab:comparison}.) Under MPC, where the identified model
enters the loop, \texttt{Ours} tracks at \SI{0.151}{\meter} RMS lateral error
against \SI{0.224}{\meter}, a \SI{32}{\percent} reduction, with maximum
\SI{0.683}{\meter} against \SI{0.776}{\meter} (Fig.~\ref{fig:trackingbox}).
ITAE is $\int t|e_{y}|\,\mathrm{d}t$, neither scale-free nor comparable across
runs of different length, and these runs are \SI{580.4}{\second} and
\SI{554.6}{\second} long, so Table~\ref{tab:comparison} reports it beside two
normalised forms from the same samples, $\mathrm{IAE}/T$ and
$\mathrm{ITAE}/(T^{2}/2)$. All three order the runs alike and at the same
magnitude, so the normalisation does not carry the result. The ITAE split
confirms that
the whole difference sits in the MPC phase: the pure-pursuit phases, which do
not use the identified model, give \num{110} and \num{107}, within
\SI{3}{\percent} of each other and two orders of magnitude smaller. The signed
error distribution (supplementary material) shows the mechanism as a narrower
distribution about a comparable centre, sample standard deviation
\SI{0.256}{\meter} against \SI{0.318}{\meter} and mean \SI{0.021}{\meter}
against \SI{0.024}{\meter}.

The QP cost is unchanged: RMS and worst-case solve times are two orders of
magnitude below the \SI{50}{\milli\second} control period and both slightly in
the baseline's favour, by \SI{5}{\percent} and \SI{1}{\percent}. The identified
coefficients change the model the QP carries, not its size.

Lap time is not read as a result: timing runs on the ROS wall clock while the
simulator advances its fixed step faster than real time, at a ratio that
differed between the runs, so the lap times in Table~\ref{tab:comparison} are
not simulated lap times and their \SIrange{5}{6}{\percent} spread is not a
vehicle-performance difference.

\subsection{Baseline pipeline versus this work}
\label{subsec:baselinecmp}

The supplementary material tabulates the pipeline as
published~\cite{ontrack2025} against the pipeline as it stands here, on every
axis that differs. The residual model, the data budget, the four stages and the

\subsection{Peak-friction warm start on a synthetic plant}
\label{subsec:mu}

brush tires rather than in CARLA, because only a synthetic plant supplies a
$\mu$ that is known exactly and an excitation level that can be swept

\SI{40}{\second} at \SI{50}{\hertz} with true $\mu=0.70$, the same effective

\subsection{What carries the result}
\label{subsec:ablations}

The two arms differ in five settings at once, so that comparison establishes an
ordering and attributes nothing. This subsection attributes it, at the cost of
leaving the loop: every number below is an \emph{offline replay} of the
identification stage on recorded buffers.

\subsubsection{Protocol}
\texttt{nn\_train} is replayed on the five \SI{30}{\second} identification
buffers each arm collected during its own run, and each configuration is scored
with the quantity the closed-loop tire-force benchmark itself reports: the
identified Magic Formula evaluated at the plant's own measured slip angles,
against its own measured lateral forces, summed per axle. No synthetic plant
and no re-derived metric are involved, so these numbers and
Table~\ref{tab:comparison}'s are on the same scale. One caveat for anyone
reproducing this from the exported curve-validation files: the lateral force
they carry is \emph{per wheel} while the vertical load beside it is the
\emph{axle} static load, and fitting them as written returns every cornering
stiffness a factor of two low.

\subsubsection{Damping the co-identification loop}
Table~\ref{tab:beta} sweeps $\beta$ of \eqref{eq:relax} at six iterations
throughout; two columns are the argument. At $\beta=\num{1.00}$, the inherited
undamped update, axle force RMSE is \num{70.4} and \SI{62.3}{\newton}, reached
by degrading monotonically from \num{38.9} and \SI{26.3}{\newton} at a single
iteration: the loop is not refining the estimate, it is amplifying its own
extrapolation. At $\beta=\num{0.00}$ the coefficients never move from the
pinned prior --- no identification at all --- and that is the \emph{worst}
column, \num{92.5} and \SI{71.8}{\newton}. The minimum sits between them at
$\beta=\num{0.20}$ (\SI{23.5}{\newton} $\pm$ \SI{1.5}{\newton} over seeds
\num{0}, \num{3} and \num{7}), and the identified $BCD$ rises monotonically
with $\beta$ across the sweep, so the damping trades how much of each fit is
accepted against how much extrapolation error comes with it rather than simply
interpolating toward the prior. Six iterations are therefore defensible on
evidence, but only damped.

\subsubsection{Pinning the peak factor}
Replayed on five buffers of a $\mu=\num{1.05}$ run, letting the fit move $D$
gives a mean $|D-\mu|$ of \num{0.585} and a maximum of \num{0.950}, with the
identified front stiffness spread over \SI{32.9}{\kilo\newton\per\radian}
across the five. Pinning only the axles whose brush fit was released reduces
those to \num{0.191}, \num{0.378} and \num{32.3}; additionally holding the
axles with no released measurement, the shipped rule, reduces them to
\num{0.084}, \num{0.213} and \SI{18.5}{\kilo\newton\per\radian}. The middle row
is the informative one: releasing an axle helps only where the gate fires, and
what removes most of the remaining error is refusing to let an unmeasured $D$
be fitted at all. The same replay is the source of the $BCD$ degeneracy
measurement quoted in Section~\ref{subsec:bcd}.

\subsubsection{Normalising the physics-informed objective}
The unnormalised form of \eqref{eq:pinnloss} puts \SI{99.9996}{\percent} of the
objective on the dynamics-residual term at initialisation against
\SI{0.0004}{\percent} on the data; with the $T_{s}/m$ and $T_{s}/I_{z}$ scaling
of \eqref{eq:physnorm} the shares become \SI{1.47}{\percent} and
\SI{98.53}{\percent}. The correction is necessary but not sufficient alone:
applied without the damping it makes the force metric slightly \emph{worse}
(\num{77.1} and \SI{76.2}{\newton} against \num{70.4} and \SI{62.3}{\newton}),
because an objective that now fits the data also supplies a sharper trajectory
for the undamped loop to amplify. With the damping in place the two arrive at
\num{28.0} and \SI{19.4}{\newton} and $R^{2}=\num{0.986}$ at the front, against
\num{92.5} and \SI{71.8}{\newton} and \num{0.870} for the unidentified prior.

\subsubsection{Two settings that do less than claimed}
Two of the five settings of Table~\ref{tab:scenarios} deserve a weaker
statement than the closed-loop comparison alone would support. The S4D residual
is worth \SI{21}{\percent} on the force metric at matched settings and a single
iteration, \SI{35.3}{\newton} against the baseline MLP's \SI{44.7}{\newton} as
a mean over the two axles --- the ablation the earlier version of this work
listed as unrun, positive in sign, but an offline replay and not a closed-loop
result. The residual \emph{objective} is a wash: with the normalisation and the
damping both in place, \texttt{physics\_informed} and plain \texttt{mse} are
indistinguishable at \SI{23.7}{\newton} each. We keep the physics-informed
objective because it costs nothing and is the paper's stated formulation, and
do not claim it buys accuracy on this manoeuvre, because it measurably does
not.

\subsection{Identification cost}
\label{subsec:runtime}

One S4D identification, six co-identification iterations on a \SI{30}{\second}
lap, takes \SI{20.9}{\second} end to end, inside the \SI{30}{\second}
re-identification period. The reduction from the \SI{181.4}{\second} it
originally took is exact: no epoch count, iteration count, early-stopping rule
or loss weight changed, and the identified coefficients agree to four decimal
places. The per-phase breakdown, the device split, and the caveat that the
stage micro-benchmarks and the end-to-end totals are separate measurements
rather than a decomposition of each other, are in the supplementary material.
