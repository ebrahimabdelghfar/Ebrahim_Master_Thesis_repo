\begin{abstract}
On-track system identification promises tire models obtained from ordinary
racing laps instead of dedicated steady-state manoeuvres. This paper studies
the identifiability of one such residual-learning pipeline~\cite{ontrack2024}
at full vehicle scale, on a simulated plant that exposes its own per-wheel slip
angle, normal load and lateral force, so an on-track estimator can be scored
against the forces the plant actually generated. Transplanted unchanged from a
small-scale platform, the pipeline converges on a tire whose peak grip is less
than half the vehicle's, with most Pacejka coefficients resting on their box
constraints. We show this is neither a solver, a bounds nor a tuning failure
but a loss of excitation created by the pipeline's own virtual-data step: the
friction the synthetic rollout can demand,
$\mu_{\mathrm{reach}}=v^{2}\delta_{\mathrm{sweep}}/(gL)$, is a property of the
rollout configuration and not of the tire. We contribute this identifiability
bound and an excitation-aware rollout; an extrapolation bound and a
frozen-prior regularisation term that jointly stabilise the peak factor across
co-identification iterations; measurement-side corrections and a friction warm
start that recovers the peak from the \emph{curvature} of the axle force curve
using inertial and odometry data alone, applied only as a floor; admissibility
gates on derived physical quantities at the model-to-controller boundary; and
an exact restructuring of the temporal residual's evaluation that takes one
identification from \SI{181}{\second} to \SI{21}{\second} without moving an
identified coefficient. After correction, live identifications return no
coefficient on a bound, peak friction and axle cornering stiffnesses within
\SI{20}{\percent} of ground truth, and one-step prediction $R^{2}$ of
\num{0.97} for lateral velocity and \num{0.99} for yaw rate. The corrected
identifier also makes a negative result visible: the optimised raceline used in
this study is infeasible for the vehicle that was measured, and the
admissibility gate rejects deployment---correctly.
\end{abstract}
