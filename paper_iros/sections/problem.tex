\section{Identification Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

The identification model is the dynamic single-track model of the baseline,
with mass $m$, yaw inertia $I_{z}$ and centre-of-gravity distances $l_{f}$,
$l_{r}$, $L=l_{f}+l_{r}$; longitudinal and lateral dynamics are decoupled and
load transfer is neglected:
\begin{align}
\dot v_{y} &= \tfrac{1}{m}\big(\Fyr+\Fyf\cos\delta - m v_{x}\omega\big),\label{eq:vydot}\\
\dot\omega &= \tfrac{1}{I_{z}}\big(\Fyf l_{f}\cos\delta - \Fyr l_{r}\big),\label{eq:omdot}
\end{align}
with slip angles
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{f}=\delta-\arctan\!\Big(\tfrac{v_{y}+l_{f}\omega}{v_{x}}\Big),\quad
\alpha_{r}=-\arctan\!\Big(\tfrac{v_{y}-l_{r}\omega}{v_{x}}\Big),
\label{eq:slip}
\end{equation}
and axle forces from the lateral Magic Formula
\begin{equation}
F_{y}=F_{z}\,D\sin\!\big(C\arctan\!\big(B\alpha-E(B\alpha-\arctan B\alpha)\big)\big),
\label{eq:mf}
\end{equation}
with per-axle coefficients
$\phip=[B_{f},C_{f},D_{f},E_{f},B_{r},C_{r},D_{r},E_{r}]$. The state and input
are $\vecx=[v_{y},\omega]$ and $\vecu=[v_{x},\delta]$, and the nominal one-step
map $\hat\vecx_{k+1}=f(\vecx_{k},\vecu_{k},\phip)$ is discretised by explicit
Euler at $T_{s}=\SI{0.035}{\second}$. The objective is to recover $\phip$ from
a single \SI{30}{\second} segment of ordinary lap data, such that the resulting
model is both predictive and \emph{admissible} for the controller that consumes
it. Admissibility is expressed on derived quantities:
the linearised axle cornering stiffness
$C_{i}=F_{z,i}B_{i}C_{i}^{\mathrm{MF}}D_{i}$, the slope of \eqref{eq:mf} at
zero slip (writing $C^{\mathrm{MF}}$ for the shape factor to avoid a clash),
and the implied grip ceiling
\begin{equation}
a_{y}^{\max}=\big(D_{f}F_{z,f}+D_{r}F_{z,r}\big)/m.
\label{eq:gripceiling}
\end{equation}
The linear model is oversteering when $l_{f}C_{f}>l_{r}C_{r}$ and is then
open-loop unstable above the critical speed
$v_{\mathrm{crit}}=L\sqrt{C_{f}C_{r}/(m(l_{f}C_{f}-l_{r}C_{r}))}$.

\subsection{Practical identifiability under limited excitation}
\label{subsec:identifiability}

Two properties of \eqref{eq:mf} are used throughout. Its slope at zero slip is
$F_{z}BCD$, so the cornering stiffness depends on the coefficients only through
that product, and $E$ drops out. If the data never approaches the peak,
\eqref{eq:mf} linearises to $F_{y}\approx F_{z}(BCD)\alpha$ and $B$, $C$, $D$
become mutually unidentifiable. Given data that does reach the peak,
\eqref{eq:mf} is \emph{structurally} identifiable. What fails here is
\emph{practical} identifiability~\cite{raue2009practical}: under a specific
excitation the likelihood is flat along the $BCD$ direction. This is the
standard persistent-excitation condition~\cite{ljung1999sysid} written on this
model, and we claim neither the structural result nor the excitation condition
as novel. Our claim, demonstrated in Section~\ref{subsec:sweep}, is that the
baseline's virtual-data step can silently violate that condition, and that a
bounded optimiser then resolves the remaining freedom by sliding onto a box
edge. The violation surfaces as a converged fit rather than as a failure.

\subsection{Baseline pipeline}
\label{subsec:baseline}

The baseline repeats four stages~\cite{ontrack2025}. A network is trained on
the recorded lap to predict the one-step error
$e_{k}=\vecx_{k+1}-\hat\vecx_{k+1}$. The corrected model is integrated
open-loop at constant speed while the steering is ramped from $0$ to
$\delta_{\mathrm{sweep}}$, which produces synthetic quasi-steady data.
Assuming $\dot v_{y}=\dot\omega=0$ there, axle forces follow from the yaw
balance,
\begin{equation}
\Fyr=\frac{m l_{f}v_{x}\omega}{L},\qquad
\Fyf=\frac{m l_{r} v_{x}\omega}{L\cos\delta},
\label{eq:ssforces}
\end{equation}
slip angles from \eqref{eq:slip}, and $\phip$ is fitted by bounded least
squares. That fitted $\phip$ becomes the next iteration's nominal model.
The point that drives the rest of this paper is that the fit \emph{never sees
the recorded lap}. It sees the rollout. The lap enters only through the
residual network, so the excitation available to the fit is set by the rollout
configuration and not by how the vehicle was driven.
